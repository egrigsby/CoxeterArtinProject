What the picture is

Ã₂ is the symmetry group of the triangular tiling of the plane. Each element is one triangle — an alcove — and the identity is the shaded one marked e. Adding a letter g to the right of the word reflects the current alcove across its g-wall, so a word of length n is a chain of n triangles, each sharing an edge with the last.

Reduced words never revisit an alcove. If a sampled letter makes the word non-reduced, the walk visibly doubles back onto a triangle it has already been to.

What the model is

A 1-layer causal transformer (4 heads, width 256, no layer norm) trained on the 1998 ShortLex normal-form words of Ã₂ of length 1 to 36, split 30/70 into train and test. At every letter it predicts the next one, over the four classes stop, 1, 2, 3.

Several letters can legally follow a given prefix, so top-1 accuracy has a ceiling well below 1; the honest test is whether a whole generated word stays in the language.

A word ends when the model emits stop, or when it runs into the 36-letter cap that the training words respect.

Reading the panel

The bars are the model's probabilities for the next letter, before any temperature is applied. Legal means that letter keeps the word inside the normal-form language, checked against all 1998 words, not predicted. When temperature is above 0 a tick on each bar marks the distribution actually sampled from.

Length ℓ is the true Coxeter length of the prefix — how far its alcove is from e. While the word stays reduced it equals the number of letters.
